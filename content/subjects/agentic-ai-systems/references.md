# References and verification notes

The reading path below is curated against the official arXiv records, conference
pages, publisher metadata, official standards, or official documentation. DOI
metadata for arXiv records uses the Crossref-registered `10.48550/arXiv.*`
identifier. Access dates are recorded so a future reviewer can re-check links.

## Research path

1. Yao et al. (2023), “ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language
   Models.” arXiv:2210.03629. Foundational control-loop paper; read for task
   setup, action/observation traces, baselines, and limitations. Free.
2. Schick et al. (2023), “Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to
   Use Tools.” arXiv:2302.04761. Tool-use training method; free.
3. Shinn et al. (2023), “Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement
   Learning.” arXiv:2303.11366. Reflection and feedback; free.
4. Wang et al. (2023), “Plan-and-Solve Prompting.” arXiv:2305.04091. Planning
   decomposition; free.
5. Park et al. (2023), “Generative Agents.” arXiv:2304.03442. Memory and
   reflection in a simulated environment; free.
6. Wang et al. (2023), “Voyager.” arXiv:2305.16291. Open-ended embodied agent;
   free.
7. Lewis et al. (2020), “Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive
   NLP Tasks.” NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2005.11401. Grounding baseline; free.
8. NIST (2023), *AI Risk Management Framework 1.0*. Official risk vocabulary
   and governance guidance; free.
9. NIST (2024), *Generative AI Profile*. Official generative-AI risk profile;
   free.
10. ISO/IEC 42001:2023. AI management-system standard; publisher access may be
    paid. Use the official catalogue metadata, not an unverified copy.
11. OpenAI, “Function calling and structured outputs.” Official API reference;
    implementation guidance, accessed 2026-07-28.
12. Anthropic, “Building effective agents.” Official engineering guidance;
    supplementary practitioner resource, accessed 2026-07-28.

## Claims and interpretation

Research claims in the chapter are paraphrases of the listed papers. The
control-loop equations, safety invariants, evaluation suggestions, and Python
program are original educational explanations. They are not claims that any
paper guarantees production safety. The BITS brochure is the primary curriculum
source; its topic description is reproduced in the subject boundary, while the
roadmap and project contracts are explicitly supplementary interpretations.
