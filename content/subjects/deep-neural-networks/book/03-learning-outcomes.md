# Learning outcomes

The following outcomes are supplementary educational design, not official
brochure wording. By the end of the approved book, a learner should be able to:

- express neural layers as tensor transformations with explicit shapes;
- derive linear neural models, backpropagation, convolution, recurrence,
  attention, and transformer components;
- implement first-principles and framework versions with tests;
- diagnose initialization, optimization, numerical, leakage, and evaluation
  failures;
- compare regularization and optimization methods under matched budgets;
- design transfer and fine-tuning protocols under domain shift;
- report parameter, activation, time, memory, and data costs;
- distinguish expressivity, optimization, generalization, calibration,
  robustness, and responsible-use claims;
- read primary papers by separating methods, measurements, author
  interpretation, later interpretation, and local reproduction;
- connect architecture mechanics to Computer Vision, NLP, Distributed ML,
  MLOps, and AI systems without duplicating those subjects.
